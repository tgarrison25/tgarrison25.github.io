Play PackIt!

A turn-based rectangle-packing game I invented, playable in your browser. On turn t you place a rectangle of area t or t+1 on an n × n grid; in solitaire the goal is to pack the grid perfectly, and in the two-player game whoever places last wins. Solitaire and AI modes are live. The mathematics is below.

New Upper Bounds on the Mondrian Art Problem

Thomas Garrison, Chris Seiler, Aliaksei Semchankau

Preprint · arXiv:2609.01998 [math.CO] · September 2026

Abstract

We present a new upper bound on the defect of the Mondrian Art Problem. The Mondrian Art Problem asks for a partition of an n × n square with rectangles of distinct dimensions such that the difference (defect) between the largest and smallest rectangle areas is minimized. We prove that for any n × n square, there exists a partition with defect O(n5/6), improving upon the previously conjectured O(n/log n) upper bound. We also implement an algorithm that provides empirical evidence supporting our theoretical bound.

PackIt! Gamified Rectangle Packing

Thomas Garrison, Marijn J. H. Heule, Bernardo Subercaseaux

12th International Conference on Fun with Algorithms (FUN 2024) · LIPIcs vol. 291, pp. 14:1–14:19

Abstract

We present and analyze PackIt!, a turn-based game consisting of packing rectangles on an n × n grid. PackIt! can be easily played on paper, either as a competitive two-player game or in solitaire fashion. On the t-th turn, a rectangle of area t or t+1 must be placed in the grid. In the two-player format of PackIt! whichever player places a rectangle last wins, whereas the goal in the solitaire variant is to perfectly pack the n × n grid. We analyze conditions for the existence of a perfect packing over n × n, then present an automated reasoning approach that allows finding perfect games of PackIt! up to n = 50 which includes a novel SAT-encoding technique of independent interest, and conclude by proving an NP-hardness result.

Weakly Consecutive Sequences

Thomas Garrison, Chris Seiler, Andrew Knowles

Preprint · arXiv:2401.09497 [math.CO] · January 2024

Abstract

A weakly consecutive sequence (WCS) is a permutation σ of {1, …, k} such that if an integer d divides σ(i), then d also divides σ(i ± d) insofar as these are defined. The structure of weakly consecutive sequences is surprisingly rich, and it is difficult to find a formula for the number N(k) of WCS's of length k. However, for a given k we describe four starting sequences, to each of which we can apply three rules or operations to generate new WCS's. We conjecture that any WCS can be constructed by applying these rules, which depend in an intricate way on the primality of k and surrounding integers. We find bounds for N(k) by analyzing these rules.

Solving PackIt!

PackIt! is a 2-player game played on an n × n board where on the ith turn you play a rectangle of area i or i+1. As in normal play, you win if you move last. The game strategy is surprisingly complex and is unsolved for n ≥ 11. We conjecture that for games of odd board size, Player 1 wins, and for even board size, Player 2 wins. This conjecture has been verified for n < 11.

The solitaire variant is the subject of the FUN 2024 paper. Presented at SEICCGTC 2026 and in the Northeastern graduate seminar.

Tiling rectangles with other rectangles up to symmetry

Advised by James Cummings

How many ways are there to tile an m × n rectangle with rectangles of integer side length, counted up to the symmetries of the rectangle? We answer this using Pólya's formula for the fixed points together with Verrill and Smith's formula for the unrestricted count. Bounding the growth rate with eigenvalue methods — Perron–Frobenius and Fischer–Courant — we pin the growth constant γ between 2.299 and 2.31.
