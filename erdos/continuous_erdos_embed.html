The classical version is an integer — 1 if you wrote a paper with him, 2 if you wrote one with someone who did — which throws away how much you worked together. Sárkőzy's 62 joint papers and a one-off coauthor both score 1. Replacing it with 1/n for n papers, and 1+1/n for n collaborators at the next tier, spreads each tier out without letting anyone cross an integer: at n=1 the two branches give exactly 1 and exactly 2.
